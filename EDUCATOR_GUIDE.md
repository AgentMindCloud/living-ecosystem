# Educator guide — Living Ecosystem Lab (Willowbrook Wilds)

## Who it is for

Approximately age 9, allowing individual differences. Session target: **10–15 minutes** with optional free exploration. This is **practice**, not assessment or diagnosis. Do not claim validated learning gains or universal age suitability.

## Prerequisites

- Comfortable tapping/clicking buttons and reading short sentences
- Optional co-play for vocabulary (producer, consumer, herbivore, predator)
- No account, wallet, or personal data required

## Learning objectives

1. Identify **producers** and **consumers** in a coherent habitat.  
2. Connect **sunlight** and **water** to plant survival, and plants to animal survival in the model.  
3. Trace a simple **food relationship** (energy flow from food → consumer).  
4. **Predict** how changing one condition affects the model, then compare with results.  
5. Distinguish this **simplified model** from real ecosystem complexity.

## Activity walkthrough

### 1. Build a habitat
Learners assemble at least two producers, two herbivores, and one predator from the Willowbrook meadow-pond set. Checking the habitat explains relationships (not decorative placement alone). Invalid combinations get specific, non-shaming feedback and unlimited retry.

**Adapt:** More help shows a food-hint scaffold; Less help hides it.

### 2. Predict & experiment
Four starting scenarios (≥3 meaningful): sunny-drying meadow (demo), cloudy wet edge (guided), balanced boost (guided), sudden shade (fresh application). Learners change sunlight and/or water, choose a prediction about total plants, advance 1 or 3 model steps, and compare. Step notes explain what changed and why.

**Adapt:** Use Apply suggested change; stay on demo before application.

### 3. Restore balance
Struggling scenarios (drought, frogs without prey, deep shade) plus an **unfamiliar transfer** (windy dune edge). Learners diagnose, adjust resources/organisms, and step until the model shows healthier scores — recovery follows model rules, not a mystery win animation.

**Adapt:** Start with drought demo; save transfer for last.

## Co-play suggestions

See on-screen adult tips. Emphasize: plants make food; animals do not eat sunlight; arrows mean energy to the consumer. Celebrate revised predictions.

## Persistence

Progress may be stored in `localStorage` key `willowbrook-wilds-v1` on this device only. **Reset** clears it. No accounts or cloud sync.

## Differentiation

| Need | Suggestion |
|------|------------|
| Less reading | More help; focus on emoji + status badge + table |
| Seeking challenge | Transfer dune scenario; try Less help |
| Colour vision | Role text labels + table; status uses words not colour alone |
| Motor | Large tap targets; +/− buttons (no drag required) |

## Suggested session length

10–15 minutes core path; +5 minutes for free replay of experiment scenarios.

## Offline follow-up

Sketch a three-link food chain from a local park. Change only one classroom “condition” card (water/sun) and predict plant health before revealing an agreed model outcome.

## Model assumptions & limitations

- Discrete steps; identical starts + actions → identical results.  
- Plant growth limited by the lower of sunlight and water (model units 0–10).  
- Populations 0–20; never negative / infinite / NaN after clamp.  
- Tiny species set; no seasons, disease, migration, or human land use.  
- Does **not** imply a single intervention guarantees a real-world ecological result.  
- English UI; not a diagnostic instrument.

## References (reputable sources)

1. **BBC Bitesize — Food chains and ecosystems (KS2 science)** — https://www.bbc.co.uk/bitesize/topics/zbnnb9q — supports producer/consumer vocabulary and simple food-chain energy ideas for primary learners.  
2. **National Geographic Education — Ecosystem** — https://education.nationalgeographic.org/resource/ecosystem/ — supports the idea that living and non-living parts interact, and that classroom models simplify real complexity.  
3. **US EPA — Aquatic ecosystems (students & educators overview)** — https://www.epa.gov/wetlands/wetlands-factsheet-series — supports connecting water conditions to wetland/plant-based habitats (adult-facing context for the meadow-pond theme).
